Why the schedule is fair

Round 1 starts from zero. Each donor has a unit budget. The schedule plays (AB)ᵏ C in block k, for k = 1, 2, 3, …. Donors A and B alternate within every block.

C plays at rounds k(k + 2), namely 3, 8, 15, 24, …. The successive gaps are 5, 7, 9, …. All three donors play infinitely often.

Work out C’s second turn

In round 8, the other donors have placed 11/16 on X and 9/32 on Z. C’s utility is min(X, Z/2).

Putting C’s full unit on Z gives Z/2 = (9/32 + 1)/2 = 41/64. X already has 11/16 = 44/64. The entire unit is needed on Z to attain 41/64, so that allocation is C’s unique best response.

C moves 1/4 unit from X to Z. The new total is (11/16, 33/32, 41/32).

How the general proof handles unbounded gaps
  1. Take a cluster point of the individual-allocation profiles and let I be the donors already best responding there.
  2. Wait for the first subsequent update by a donor outside I. All outside allocations stay fixed until this turn. At the limit, the pre-update profile lies on the same slice and has the same potential, which forces the same aggregate.
  3. The outside donor’s gain depends only on her old allocation and the aggregate. Its limiting value would therefore be strictly positive. The actual potential increments tend to zero, so no such outside donor exists.

Every cluster profile is an equilibrium. All equilibria share the unique Nash-welfare aggregate. The theorem applies to every fair schedule and concerns the aggregate. The plot shows 13 exact totals from one example.
